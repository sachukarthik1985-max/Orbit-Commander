REAL NASA HEALTH FINDING (OSDR)

Mice that spent about a month in space on the BION-M1 satellite made fewer young germ-fighting cells (B cells) in their bones, and the change lasted at least a week after they landed. Space is hard on bodies, which is why your crew needs enough food, water and air to stay healthy.

Source: NASA Open Science Data Repository (OSDR), dataset GLDS-345 "Mouse femur LC-MSMS upon weightlessness".

SPACE WORDS

CAPCOM: The person at mission control who talks to the astronauts.
Sol: One day on Mars, about 24 hours and 40 minutes.
Life support: The machines that make air and clean water for the crew.
Solar panels: Panels that turn sunlight into electricity.
Battery: Saves power from the day so the base can run at night.
kW (kilowatt): How much power something uses or makes.
Solar flare: A huge flash from the Sun. A bigger X number means a stronger flare.
Particle storm: Tiny, fast bits from the Sun that can follow a big flare.
Radiation dose (mSv): How much space radiation the crew has soaked up in total. Lower is safer.
µSv/h: How much radiation hits the crew each hour. 1,000 µSv = 1 mSv.
kWh (kilowatt-hour): How much energy the battery has saved up.
Communication delay: Radio travels at the speed of light, but Mars is so far away that a message takes about 3 to 22 minutes to arrive.
Microgravity: Floating in space with almost no weight. It makes bones and muscles weaker.

THE SCIENCE BEHIND THE GAME

1. SOLAR POWER, DAY AND NIGHT

We sped things up: in the game a Moon day lasts 24 hours. A real day on the Moon lasts about a month (2 weeks of light, 2 weeks of dark). If the battery is empty and there is no sun, the machines stop.

P_solar = P_max · max(0, sin(2π · t / 24)) · (1 - Dust%)

Panels make most power at midday, none at night, and less when they are dusty.

2. HOW RADIATION ADDS UP
Dose_rate = (Space radiation + Flare storm) · (1 - Soil shield) · (0.1 if in shelter)

Space radiation plus any particle storm, made smaller by the soil shield and the storm shelter.

3. REAL NASA SOLAR FLARES USED IN THIS GAME 5 REAL FLARES BUILT IN

Every flare in the game is a real flare from NASA's Space Weather Database (DONKI). The five below are built into the game, so it works without internet. If the internet is on, the game also downloads the newest strong flares from DONKI. An M flare is 10 times weaker than an X flare with the same number.

Game rule (not always true in real life): in this game every flare sends a particle storm, and bigger flares send stronger storms. The storm strength is our game estimate. In real life, only some flares send particle storms.

4. REAL NASA DATA WE USED (AND WHERE IT CAME FROM)
  • Solar flares: NASA Space Weather Database (DONKI), kauai.ccmc.gsfc.nasa.gov/DONKI. Flare dates and sizes are real.
  • Mars radiation: NASA Curiosity rover's Radiation Assessment Detector measured about 0.64 mSv per day on Mars (Hassler et al., Science, 2014). In the game: 27 µSv/h.
  • Moon radiation: China's Chang'e 4 lander measured about 1.37 mSv per day on the Moon (Zhang et al., Science Advances, 2020). In the game: 57 µSv/h. They also found that about 50 cm of Moon soil on top of a base protects people, which is why the Soil Wall Shield works.
  • Astronaut health: NASA Open Science Data Repository (OSDR), dataset GLDS-345 (mice on the BION-M1 satellite).
  • Air, water and food per astronaut: NASA life-support planning numbers: 0.84 kg of oxygen per person per day.
  • Mars facts: NASA Mars Exploration: a sol is about 24 h 40 min, and Mars gets about 43% of the sunlight Earth gets (590 vs 1361 W/m²). Sites: Shackleton Rim (Artemis Moon south pole region) and Jezero Crater (Perseverance rover).
  • Radio delay: light takes about 1.3 seconds to reach the Moon and about 3 to 22 minutes to reach Mars, depending on where the planets are.
